A driving theory book for your driving school: who writes the manual and how the price is set

by Stefano Braccini, editorial coordinator · 18 November 2026 · all articles

Someone looking for a driving theory book is often a learner who has to study for the theory test, and for them the right move is to ask their driving school for the book. This article, however, is for those who run a driving school and would like to give their learners a manual of their own, with their own brand and their own way of explaining things. Here is what it contains, who writes it and how the price is worked out.

Why a manual of your own

Every instructor knows where learners get stuck: right of way at junctions, safe following distances, road signs that look alike. A manual written for your driving school explains exactly those points with the examples you use in your lessons, and learners find on the page the same words they heard from their instructor. Your brand is on the cover: the book your learners take home reminds them who taught them to drive.

What goes in it

Usually the manual follows the official syllabus and the official revision questions, one topic per chapter. Each chapter can have the explanation in short sentences, the examples that make the rules clear and, at the end, a revision sheet. Alongside the manual we can prepare explanations of the test questions, that is, the reason why each answer is right or wrong: this way learners understand the rule instead of memorising the answer.

Two things can be added:

  1. A simplified version, for those learning English as an additional language.
  2. Translations into Italian, French, German and Spanish, with two rounds of review.

Who writes it

You bring the technical content: your experience in the classroom, your way of explaining, the mistakes you see again and again. We do the writing: we put the material in order, write the chapters and send them to you as each one is ready, so that you can check them. You choose the name on the cover: the driving school, yourself or one of your instructors. The contract and the confidentiality agreement are signed before we start.

Explaining a technique one step at a time, with the right words for people who are learning, is what we do in our own manuals: our catalogue has 45 titles and 265 published editions, and among those titles are 40 technical cookery manuals. You can find an example of how we write manuals in the excerpts from our books.

When the rules change

The rules of the road change, and a manual that stands still becomes wrong. Updates are made to the book you already have: the chapters affected by the change are corrected and the new edition is prepared, without rewriting everything.

How the price is set

The price depends on how much work there is to do: a complete manual costs more than revision sheets alone or the explanations of the test questions, and the simplified versions and the languages also count. That is why the quotation, based on the work to be done and on the continuity of the collaboration, comes after the first conversation. We can also deliver the laid-out file, ready for print, and the accessible EPUB edition, which can be listened to with text-to-speech. What we write for driving schools, schools and training providers is explained on the page Commissioned textbooks and teaching materials.

If you would rather write it yourself

If you want to write the manual yourself, we can support you: an assessment of the material you already have, with a written report and an outline, costs €100, which we deduct from the quotation if the work continues with us; written mentoring while you write costs €179 a month; consultancy on the project costs €150 an hour.
