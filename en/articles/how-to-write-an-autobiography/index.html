How to write an autobiography, step by step

by Stefano Braccini, editorial coordinator · 13 November 2026 · all articles

Writing your own life looks easy: you know the facts, and you know the characters too. Then you sit down in front of the page and do not know where to begin. Here are the steps, in the order that works best, so that your autobiography makes it to the last page.

1. Decide who you are writing it for

It is the first question and it changes everything else. A book for your children and grandchildren can take the family names for granted and dwell on family memories. A book for people who do not know you has to explain who everyone is and why your story matters to them too. Write down in one line who the book is for and keep it in front of you while you work.

2. Gather the material before you write

Photographs, letters, diaries, documents, appointment diaries: put them in date order and build a timeline, year by year, with the main events. Then talk to the people who were there. A brother, an old colleague, a childhood friend remember details you have forgotten, and they often remember the same things differently: that is material too.

3. Choose the thread of the story

You do not have to start from your birth. You can follow the order of the years, you can organise the book by themes (work, family, places), or you can start from a turning point and go back. What matters is that there is a question holding the book together: how I got here, what I have learnt, what I want to leave behind.

4. Make an outline

Divide the story into chapters and for each chapter write two lines: which period it covers and which episode carries it. A one-page outline is worth more than a hundred pages written with no direction, because every day it tells you where to pick up again.

5. Tell scenes, not summaries

“My father was strict” says nothing. The day he made you redo your homework three times, with the radio on in the kitchen and dinner going cold, says it all. Choose a few episodes and tell them properly: who was there, what could be seen, what was said. Scenes are what readers remember.

6. Write in your own voice

An autobiography should not read like a school essay. Write the way you speak, in the first person. If the words will not come, record yourself telling the story out loud and then transcribe it: many of the best pages are born that way.

7. Choose what to tell about other people

Your life includes people who are still alive and will read the book. Decide beforehand what goes in and what stays out, and ask yourself how anyone who recognises themselves in those pages will feel. Not everything that is true needs to be written, and the book is the better for it.

8. Keep a rhythm and reread at a distance

Half an hour a day, every day, takes you further than a weekend every now and then. In the first draft, do not correct: keep going. When you have finished, let the text rest for a few weeks and then reread it from the start, as if someone else had written it.
