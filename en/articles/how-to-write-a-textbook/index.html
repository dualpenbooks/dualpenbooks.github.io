A commissioned textbook: how the writer works for an educational publisher

by Stefano Braccini, editorial coordinator · 12 November 2026 · all articles

Behind a textbook there is almost always a team: a publisher who decides on the project, an author (often a teacher) whose name goes on the cover, and the people who write it, revise it and lay it out. If you are wondering how a textbook is written, here is the process, who does what, and where a studio that writes on commission comes in.

It starts with what the school has to teach

In Italy a textbook follows the Ministry's documents: the Indicazioni nazionali (national guidelines) for primary and lower secondary schools and for the licei, and the Linee guida for technical and vocational institutes. When those documents change, as they have been in recent years, the books are updated or rewritten.

The first job is the annotated outline: the units, the chapters, the objectives of each lesson, the time the class really has during the year. The outline is agreed with the publisher before a single line is written, because it is the map everyone works from, from the author to the typesetter.

What a lesson is made of

A lesson has several layers, and each one has to be written. There is the explanation, with short sentences and an example for every concept. There are the diagrams, the pictures and the captions, which often teach as much as the text. There are the key words and the summary map at the end. Then the exercises, from the easiest to the hardest, and the tests. Separately, the answers and the teacher's guide.

The language is measured against the age of the pupils: a history chapter for the first year of lower secondary school is written differently from one for the final year of upper secondary. And today the book almost always has a digital part too (interactive exercises, audio, video), with an EPUB version that must be accessible to anyone who listens to it with text-to-speech.

Who does what

The publisher chooses the project, the audience and the schedule. The calendar is set by the schools: in Italy teachers choose their books in May, for the following year, and the book has to reach their desks before then.

The author brings the subject and classroom experience. They know where pupils get stuck and which example gets them moving again.

The editorial team checks the facts, makes the language consistent across chapters and redoes the exercises one by one, to make sure the answers are right.

Then come the design, the layout and the digital version.

Where a commissioned writer comes in

Sometimes a publisher has the project but not the author, or has an excellent teacher who cannot deliver on time. Then the text is written by a studio working for third parties, and it is published under the name of the author the publisher has chosen.
