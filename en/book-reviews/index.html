Your review, on the date you choose

DualPen Books reviews other authors' books. You choose when it goes live: four weeks, two, or one. We put that date in writing and we keep it.

Why the date matters more than it seems

Asking for a review usually means writing to a blog, getting a kind reply, and then waiting. Sometimes it arrives, sometimes it doesn't, and hardly ever when you need it. It is not bad faith: people who review out of love for books do it in whatever time is left over, and they cannot promise anything to anyone. They say so openly, and they are right to.

The trouble is that you do have a date. The release, the fair, the launch event, Christmas. A review that lands three months later is no longer a launch tool, it is a souvenir.

We put the date in writing. That is what you are paying for, and it is the only thing the price buys: time and work. Never the verdict.

What an editorial review is

It is not one more star on your book's page. It is a signed critical judgement, written by people who read the whole book and assess it the way a book is assessed: structure, pace, characters or argument, language, how the whole thing holds together. Praise where praise is due, criticism where it is needed.

It does two jobs. It gives you an authoritative sentence to put where it counts: the Editorial Reviews section of your Amazon page, your website, the cover, social media. And it gives a reader who has never heard of you a reason to trust the book that isn't your own word for it.

Who writes it

Not a blog run by keen readers. That is a different trade, a respectable one, and it is not ours.

We are an editorial studio: 290 editions in catalogue, 40 technical manuals, a series published in 14 languages, a historical saga in ten novels. When we read your book we recognise the work that went into it and the work that is missing, because that work is what we do every day. And we know what an author actually needs, because we have been on the other side of the table for years.

We have put our own books through the same judgement we ask you to accept: the saga «A Mari Usque Ad Mare» has been read and reviewed by independent English-language critics. Their reviews are public, on the home page of this site, with the scores they gave.

What you get

  • The full review, published here on a page of its own, with a permanent address you can link anywhere, and it stays there.
  • A short extract, ready to use, to paste into the Editorial Reviews section of your Amazon page.
  • The same review in a second language, your pick of our five, translated through our double-revision pipeline: useful the day your book has an edition there.
  • An image card with the cover, the score and the best line, ready for social media and for your own site. It is the same card we make for our own books.
  • A star rating, from one to five.

How it works

  1. You write to us. Title, genre, page count, the link to your book page if it is already on sale, and the first chapter in Word or PDF.
  2. We read the first chapter and tell you whether we are taking it on. If it isn't for us you will know straight away, and you have paid nothing.
  3. If we take it on, you choose the date. Once payment is through, you send us the complete book in EPUB or PDF.
  4. We read the whole thing. Then we write.
  5. We publish by the agreed date and send you the link, the extract, the translation and the card.

The rules we hold ourselves to

You pay for time and work, not for the score.

No sum and no arrangement can change what we write. If a service promises you a positive review in exchange for money, change service: it is selling you something worthless and putting you at risk.

Below four stars, you decide.

If the judgement is harsh we send it to you before publishing and you choose whether it goes out. The text is never altered: the only thing that changes is whether it is published or stays in the drawer.

We always declare the copy we received.

At the foot of every review it says that the book was sent to us by the author or the publisher, and that the review was commissioned.

We do not review our own books.

Our editions never appear here. We have them judged by other people, like everyone else.

We post nothing under our own name on your Amazon page.

Store rules forbid reviews from anyone with a financial interest in the book, and we have one, because you paid us. You are the one who puts the extract in the Editorial Reviews section, which is the place designed for it and breaks no rule. Anyone offering you reviews uploaded under their own name onto your product page is putting your book and your account at risk.

What we read and what we don't

We read fiction and non-fiction in all five of our languages: English, Italian, French, German and Spanish. Self-published, traditionally published, hybrid. Advance copies too, provided they have already been edited.

We do not read erotica, texts that attack people or groups, or manuscripts that have not been edited yet. We do not review audiobooks.

We may also say no without explaining why. A no is not a verdict on your book: it means it isn't for us.

Who signs

Reviews are signed DualPen Books, the editorial team. It is how our editorial team works. Each book goes to the pen best suited to its genre, and what comes out is the studio's best judgement.
