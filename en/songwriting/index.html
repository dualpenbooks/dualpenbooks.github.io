We write for people who make music

You have a beat, an idea, a story to tell. The right words take craft: a metre that lands, rhymes that sound natural, a hook that stays in the head after one listen. We write the words, you bring the voice.

Song lyrics

A single track over the beat you have already chosen, or a whole project: EP, album, concept album with a thread running through it. Any genre.

The lyrics reach you split bar by bar, marked with where each section comes in over your beat. You take them straight into the booth, no reworking needed.

The lyrics you have already written

You have put something down and you want it brought up to standard. We keep your substance and rebuild the form: metre, rhyme, hook. It stays your track, and it sounds the way it should.

If you have changed beat and the lyrics no longer fit, we cut them to the new tempo.

Singable translation

Your track in another language: Italian, English, French, German or Spanish, with the metre intact and the rhymes in place.

Translating a song means making sense, stress and rhyme all work at once. It is a craft of its own, and it is ours. If you want to be heard beyond your own market, this is where it starts.

Your biography

The three bios you get asked for every time, written properly: the long one for journalists, the short one for the night’s line-up, and the 150-character one for Spotify and social.

It is the calling card that travels instead of you, and almost nobody has had it properly written.

Press kit for artists (EPK)

When you approach a venue, a festival, a label, a press officer or a playlist curator, the first thing they ask for is your press kit. We write the whole thing:

  • The three biographies: long, short and 150 characters
  • A presentation of the project or the record
  • A sheet for every track
  • The press release for the release
  • Questions and answers ready for interviews, so you arrive prepared
  • The practical notes: line-up, contacts, where to listen

The photos and videos are yours. The words are ours.

And then

Press releases for a single release. Liner notes and booklet texts. Anthems, jingles and lyrics for companies, towns and events. A critical read of your own lyrics, with a written report telling you what works and what to work on.

How we work

  1. We talk. You write to us about what you have in mind. The first exchange is free and without obligation.
  2. The brief. We ask you the right questions: where you come from, how you speak, what you want to say. This is the step that makes the difference between your lyrics and any lyrics.
  3. We write. Over your beat, at your tempo, in your voice.
  4. We fix it together. Revisions are included in the price and written into the contract.
  5. Delivery. The final lyrics, ready to sing.

The lyrics are yours

On delivery the rights pass entirely to you. You can register them in your own name with a collecting society, record them, publish them, assign them to a label. They go out under your name, yours alone.

Confidentiality is total, and it is written into the contract.

Questions

Do you write the music as well, or only the words?

Our craft is the written word. We deliver lyrics ready to sing, cut to the metre of your beat and split bar by bar, marked with where each section comes in. You bring the voice, in a studio or at home.

How much does it cost to have a song written?

It depends on the project: a single verse and an album are two different jobs. Tell us in a couple of lines what you have in mind and we come back with a figure and a date. The first exchange is free and without obligation.

Do the lyrics become mine? Can I register them in my own name?

Yes. On delivery the rights pass entirely to you: you can register the track in your own name as sole author of the lyrics, record it, publish it and assign it to a label. It goes out under your name, yours alone, and confidentiality is written into the contract.

Do you translate songs into other languages?

Yes, into Italian, English, French, German and Spanish, with the metre intact and the rhymes in place. It is a different job from translating ordinary text, because sense, stress and rhyme all have to work at once.

What do you need to start?

Your beat, if you have already chosen one, and half an hour of your time for the brief: where you come from, how you speak, what you want to say. That is the step that makes the difference between your lyrics and any lyrics.
